What is a shot on target?

A shot is any attempt to score. It counts as on target when it is heading into the goal, so it either goes in or the goalkeeper has to stop it.

Shots that fly wide, hit the post or get blocked by a defender are shots, but not shots on target.

Every extra shot on target raises the odds of winning

Each bar groups matches by how many more shots on target a team had than its opponent. Teams level on shots on target win just under four in ten. With three more than the opponent, they win two in three.

Possession matters over a season, not in a single match

Over a full season, teams that have more of the ball finish higher in the table. In a single match the link almost disappears.

The reason is the score. A team that goes ahead often lets the other side have the ball and defends its lead. So the winner of a match frequently ends up with less possession, even though better teams have more of it over a season.

What teams can take from this

Take better shots

Shots on target predict wins far better than total shots. Work the ball into the box and shoot from good positions instead of trying from distance.

Use the ball to create chances

Teams with more of the ball don't win single matches much more often. Passing should move the ball forward and lead to shots.

Keep eleven players on the pitch

Red cards hurt results in all three competitions. Playing a player down makes winning much harder.

Make home games count

Home teams won 48% of matches and away teams 29%, a gap of about 20 points in every competition. Attack with confidence at home.

These are patterns in the data, not guaranteed causes. Teams with better players tend to get more shots on target anyway.

How the analysis works

Data. Match stats from ESPN for the 2022/23 Premier League, La Liga and Champions League (group stage onward). ESPN has no stats for 108 matches and 6 more can't produce ratio stats, which leaves 293 Premier League, 351 La Liga and 123 Champions League matches. Each match is seen from both teams' side.

Approach. Every stat is turned into a difference: the team minus its opponent. Goals, assists and saves are left out because they give away the score. Total passes is dropped because it moves almost exactly with possession (r = 0.96).

Methods. Single-stat correlations and win rates; a standardised logistic regression and a random forest scored by five-fold cross-validated AUC; and season averages for each team against points per game, which removes most of the effect of the score on the stats.

A model using only match stats

Standardised logistic regression coefficients: the effect of a one-standard-deviation increase in each difference, holding the others fixed.

AUC measures how well the model separates wins from non-wins: 0.5 is a coin toss, 1.0 is perfect.
